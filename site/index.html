Terminals that keep running.

Belay holds your terminal sessions where they run: on your computer, and on every server you work on over ssh. Close Belay, lose the wifi, shut the lid. Claude keeps working on the server, and Belay comes back to exactly where it was.

Download for free macOS and Linux. Nothing to install on your servers.

How it works

A holder, where the session runs

Every session belongs to a small holder process, not to the window you watch it in. On a server, Belay puts its holder there itself, over ssh: one file, no Python, no Node, nothing to install.

Belay comes and goes

Belay is only watching. When it closes, or the connection drops, the holder keeps the session running and remembers everything it printed.

Back exactly where it was

When Belay reconnects, it asks for everything after the last byte it saw. Nothing is missed, nothing comes twice, and long-running agents never notice you were gone.

Questions

What do my servers need?

An ssh login with your key or ssh agent, a shell, and a home directory Belay can write to. Linux (x86-64 or ARM) or macOS. Belay puts its holder in ~/.belay there; delete the directory and it is gone.

Do I still need tmux?

No. Belay's holder does what tmux does for keeping sessions alive, and Belay's window does the layout: splits, tabs and scrollback that scrolls like a normal terminal.

What about a server that asks for a password?

Belay can't keep a holder there, so it runs the session over plain ssh instead, where you can type the password, and marks it not held: that session ends if the connection drops.

Where do my sessions run when they are on my computer?

In Belay's holder on your computer, so quitting or updating Belay doesn't end them. Restarting the computer does, as with any terminal; Belay starts them again where they were.

Does it cost anything?

No. Belay is free.
